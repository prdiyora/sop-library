AI Visibility

When someone asks an AI app about your niche, does your website show up in the answer? Three parts: find the right competitors, research the questions with Semrush, then set up the business properly inside ChatGPT. Simple flow first, numbered steps under it.

Read this first. Nothing in this SOP buys anything, spends credits, submits an ID or publishes a page on its own. Every outward step needs its own approval first. Numbers below are research targets and examples, never promises of AI visibility.

Part A — Semrush research

Find the questions people ask AI apps where your website is missing, then prepare original pages that answer them. Pick the competitors for this part from Part C's final list.

Part A at a glance — 5 steps

1

▶Pick your website, country and AI app

Open Semrush's AI Toolkit, type in the exact domain, set the filters once and write them down.

2

▤Write down today's numbers

Save the starting score: visibility, mentions, cited pages. Same filters every time, or the comparison lies.

3

⌕Find questions people ask AI

Topic Opportunities lists real questions, how often they're asked, and how hard each is to win.

4

⊘Find questions competitors answer but you don't

Competitor Research > Missing prompts. Each gap is a page idea.

5

✎Write your own page, then check again

Draft an original, fact-checked page. Get approval, publish, and re-check with the same filters after 7 days.

  2. Goal: Find questions where the website is missing from AI answers, then prepare useful, original pages that answer those questions. This SOP explains research and preparation. It is not permission to buy access, use paid credits or publish pages.

Before starting

  1. Confirm the project domain, target audience, country and language. Use the project's agreed market; use Worldwide only when no specific market is required.
  2. Confirm the Semrush account and approved access route. The screen names below follow the user's Semrush notes. This SOP has not been checked in live Semrush. If a label or metric is unclear, mark it "Verify in Semrush" and ask instead of guessing.
  3. Confirm access, any credit cost and the scope of approval. If these are unclear, pause the affected action and ask. Do not switch accounts or spend credits without approval.
  4. Create a dated research record. Keep the original notes unchanged. Record the domain, country, language, platform filters, report date and tool data-update date.

Example used in this SOP

  1. A home-organization website targets the US. A candidate question is "How do I make a weekly cleaning schedule?" Replace the website, market and question with the real project. All examples are illustrative, not live research results.

  2. The user's strategy: Work through all Topic Opportunities and competitor Missing prompts, capture the full response and website sources for research, then build pages that cover those questions. The steps below preserve that research flow. Added safeguards distinguish private research from publishable content, require original writing and flag overlap before a new page is created.

Expected outputs

  1. A baseline record, a deduplicated opportunity list, a source-backed brief for each selected page, an approved publishing record and a seven-day review. If the tool has no usable data, report that result instead of inventing metrics or topics.

2. Record the baseline

  1. Purpose: Save a consistent starting point so later comparisons use the same domain and filters.

  2. Open AI Visibility > Visibility Overview. Enter the exact project domain.

  3. Select the agreed country and platform filters. Record them before collecting numbers.

  4. Record the visibility score and the displayed metrics: Mentions, Citations and Cited Pages. Include any change values, comparison period and data-update date shown.

  5. Open the visibility breakdown. Record the overall score and each available platform separately, such as ChatGPT, AI Overview, AI Mode or Gemini. Do not assume a platform exists if the tool does not show it.

  6. Record Distribution by LLM and Mentions by Country. Preserve the displayed units, values and report links or screenshots.

  7. Note missing or unavailable reports as "Not available". Use zero only when the tool explicitly reports zero.

How to read the metrics safely

  1. Use Semrush's current definitions (verify in Semrush). A citation count is not automatically a count of unique website pages. Keep Citations and Cited Pages separate. Do not treat a score change as proof that one page caused it.

Baseline record fields

  1. Project/domain; research date; tool/account label; country; language; platform; data-update date; comparison period; visibility score; mentions; citations; cited pages; platform breakdown; country breakdown; evidence link; notes.

Baseline completion check

  1. Confirm the domain is correct, all filters are visible in the evidence, and every recorded number has a label. Example numbers from the original notes must not be reused as the project's results.

3. Method A: Topic opportunities

  1. Purpose: Find relevant questions where the project's current coverage can improve.

  2. Nearby tabs in the source notes: Your Performing Topics, Topic Opportunities, Cited Sources, Source Opportunities and Cited Pages. The working path here is Topic Opportunities. Verify exact spelling and availability in Semrush.

  3. Open Topics & Sources > Topic Opportunities. Keep the same project and market filters.

  4. Record the topic, competitors mentioned, AI volume and intent exactly as displayed. Treat volume as the tool's estimate, not a promise of visits.

  5. Open a relevant topic and inspect its prompts. For each useful prompt, open View full response.

  6. Record the exact prompt, country, platform, last-update date, brands mentioned, whether the project is mentioned and whether its domain is cited.

  7. Write a short summary of the answer's questions, subtopics and missing details. Keep an evidence link or permitted private reference. Do not paste the full answer into a publishable draft.

  8. Collect the cited website URLs. Open each useful source and check that it actually supports the proposed claim. Skip social-media sources for this workflow. A search homepage alone is not evidence for a factual claim.

  9. Check the project's existing pages. Decide whether to improve an existing page, combine overlapping ideas or propose a genuinely new page.

  10. Prepare an original content brief using section 5. Prioritize relevance, user value and evidence quality. Record every opportunity and process the full list. As an added quality safeguard, flag irrelevant or duplicate prompts for review rather than blindly creating separate pages for them.

Worked example

  1. Prompt: "How do I make a weekly cleaning schedule?"

  2. Research notes: Identify useful subtopics such as available time, room frequency and a sample routine. Check reliable website sources and the project's existing cleaning guide.

  3. Original brief: A realistic weekly plan for small homes, with a new worked example and editable checklist. Do not reproduce a competitor's schedule, prose or design.

Stop or escalate when

  1. The prompt is outside the site's purpose, the evidence cannot be checked, the topic duplicates an existing page, or the content needs expertise the project cannot supply. Record the reason and continue with independent, suitable topics.

4. Method B: Competitor missing prompts

  1. Purpose: Find relevant questions where selected competitors appear but the project does not.

  2. Open Competitor Research. Select the agreed country and All platforms if available. Record the selected filters.

  3. Enter the project domain under You. Add up to four relevant competitor domains. If competitors are not already agreed, propose them with reasons instead of treating any ranking site as an approved competitor.

  4. Record the displayed visibility score for each domain, the comparison period and data-update date. Do not mix this all-platform comparison with a single-platform baseline.

  5. Open Topics & Prompts, choose the Prompts view and then Missing from All / Missing / Shared / Unique. If the interface differs, locate the equivalent report and record the difference.

  6. For each relevant missing prompt, open the full response. Record the prompt, platform, country, update date, mentioned brands, citation status and website source URLs.

  7. Use the same evidence checks as Method A. Summarize coverage; do not copy the response or competitor wording. Skip Instagram, YouTube, Facebook and other social-platform sources in this workflow.

  8. Deduplicate against Method A and existing site pages. Assign a decision: improve existing page, propose new page, merge with another topic, or reject with a reason.

  9. Send the selected opportunities and briefs for review before any publishing action.

Decision example

  1. If competitors appear for "weekly cleaning schedule for a small apartment" and the project already has a general cleaning guide, first check whether a useful apartment section belongs there. A new page is justified only when it serves a distinct need with enough original value.

One row per opportunity

  1. ID; method; topic; exact prompt; intent; country; platform; tool update date; competitor domains; project mentioned/cited; website sources; existing page; proposed action; priority and reason; brief link; approval/status.

5. Create, review and measure

Prepare the page brief

  1. State the target reader, their question and the page's purpose. Choose an existing page to improve or propose a new page.
  2. Write an original outline. Cover useful questions found during research, but omit irrelevant or unsupported claims. There is no requirement to include every line of an AI answer.
  3. Attach checked website sources to the claims they support. Prefer primary evidence where available. Verify factual, product and health claims; seek expert review when needed.
  4. Add project-specific value: a new example, tested process, clear comparison, original checklist or first-hand evidence. Do not invent experience, results, credentials or product features.
  5. Draft the title, headings, concise answer and internal-link suggestions. Write in the site's voice. Do not copy AI answers, competitor prose, images, tables or distinctive layouts.
  6. Check factual accuracy, readability, source attribution, links, originality and overlap with existing pages. Obtain the required approval before publishing or changing a live page.

After an approved publication

  1. Record the actual live URL and publication date. Arrange a review for seven days later, using the same domain, market, platform and prompt filters. A review is not complete merely because a reminder exists.

  2. At the review, check whether the tool has refreshed its data. Record mentions, citations, cited pages, visibility and the relevant prompt evidence. Separate "No change in refreshed data" from "Data has not refreshed" and "Not available". If results are unchanged, diagnose relevance, evidence, technical accessibility and content usefulness before adding more pages.

Rules that must stay with this SOP

  • No citation guarantee: The source notes' approximate 90% claim is not a verified success rate and must not be used as a forecast.
  • Research is not permission: Account access, paid credits, publishing and sharing follow the user's actual approval.
  • Report honestly: Do not present sample metrics as live results or a seven-day review as proof of causation.
  1. Source and revision: Reorganized from the user's original AI visibility notes. Original unchanged. This version adds explicit evidence checks, originality rules and approval gates; tool labels remain subject to live verification.

Purpose and evidence

  1. Part A (sections 1-5) covers Semrush research. Part B (sections 6-9) organizes the original document's Tab 2: business claiming, profile management, feeds, insights and an optional customer-facing Business Agent. This is not the assistant reading this SOP, and it is not a guarantee of AI visibility.

  2. Official OpenAI setup guidance and Business Agents Policy were checked on 18 November 2026. No Business Hub account, store or live claim was opened. Features and eligibility must be checked again before execution.

Eligibility: choose the right path

  1. India-based business: A claim may be available where ChatGPT is offered and verification is supported, depending on location and business type. Do not promise that every Indian business can claim or access every feature. Use section 7 only if the actual listing offers a supported route.

  2. US business: Business Agents currently require an eligible US ecommerce or online-service business with a website, plus approved claim and Persona verification. US registration or US customers alone do not establish eligibility. A verified profile does not automatically qualify for an agent.

  3. Shopify is optional, not a prerequisite for claiming or for an eligible agent. Targeting US buyers does not by itself turn an India-based business into an eligible US business. Represent the actual business and representative honestly.

Before any action

  1. Confirm the exact legal business, trade name, website, location, owner/authorized representative and ChatGPT account to manage it. Use real records, not the example business in any notes.

  2. Confirm whether the goal is research only, a claim, profile changes, a feed or an optional agent. Obtain separate authority for the actual submissions, disclosures and publication.

  3. Gather only required information through the appropriate secure process: official business name/address, website, contact channels, registration records and proof of representation. Government ID may be requested by Persona; never put it or credentials into this SOP, ordinary chat or a research sheet.

  4. Warn before creating a new profile through Persona: OpenAI says approval automatically publishes that profile, with no separate profile-publish step. Its setup guide also says Support cannot delete, unpublish or hide a published profile as part of a setup/ownership request. Stop for explicit approval before that flow.

7. Claim and manage a business

  1. Use this common path for an eligible India-based or US business. Available verification methods vary by listing. Do not force the Shopify route or invent a missing option.

Claim and verification steps

  1. Sign in with the agreed ChatGPT account. Open Business Hub from the sidebar, or the entry URL listed in section 9. Search for the exact business name and match both website and brand.

  2. Select the correct listing and confirm the managing account. If an eligible online business is not found, use Add business only if offered. Local stores/restaurants currently can claim only an existing US listing. If the listing is locked or needs a tenant administrator, use the stated support/admin route; do not bypass it.

  3. Choose one verification method offered: business phone, business email, website domain or Shopify. Inspect the recipient/store/domain before proceeding. A verification attempt does not mean access has been approved.

  4. Phone/email: request the code only for the correct business contact and complete the offered flow. Domain: with approval, add the exact TXT type, host/name and value shown to the correct DNS zone. Preserve unrelated records, allow propagation, then select Verify domain. Do not replace existing records to make verification pass.

  5. Shopify, if selected: choose Connect Shopify, inspect requested permissions, sign in to the correct store and authorize only the approved connection. Do not connect another client's store. Connecting Shopify alone does not publish a Business Agent.

  6. Wait for the claim decision email or request for information. Recover the claim under the same managing account. If pending, wait rather than making duplicate submissions. A failed verification follows the retry/support path actually offered.

  7. After claim approval, open Business > correct profile > Complete verification with Persona / Verify with Persona > Continue to Persona. With explicit approval, submit only requested identity, registration and authorization information through the verified flow. Use the legal name/address from records and the trade name only in a separate trade-name field.

  8. Wait for Persona approval. A submitted ID is not approval. Persona is required before publishing business-information changes, viewing insights, connecting a product feed or setting up an eligible agent. A blue check follows Persona approval according to the detailed setup guide.

Profile and catalog steps

  1. After both approvals, prepare accurate name, website, description, category, logo, cover image, delivery/return policies and support information. Obtain approval for the actual content and any new audience, then submit supported changes. OpenAI reviews/publishes them; saved changes may not appear immediately.

  2. For products, inspect the offered feed options and eligibility. Shopify, a compliant CSV upload or a hosted feed URL may be supported. Match the profile website to product domains and check names, prices, currency, availability and links before submission. A feed neither publishes an agent nor guarantees recommendations.

  3. Record claim/profile/feed status, approval evidence and the next required decision. Do not call a profile verified or a feed current until that state is confirmed in the correct account.

8. Optional US Business Agent

  1. Use this section only after the business meets current location, type, industry and website requirements and both claim and Persona approvals are confirmed. Availability of a profile is not agent eligibility. Do not use a VPN, false address or another business's registration to qualify.

Create and prepare the draft

  1. Open Business > approved business profile. If Shopify setup created a draft agent, inspect that draft. Otherwise use Create agent only if available. If absent, check eligibility and verification status or use Support; do not assume a hidden workaround.

  2. Review the business facts the agent will use: introduction, website, categories, brand guidance and actual products/services. For products, check descriptions, prices, availability and supported ingredients/usage claims where relevant. Include only information needed for this business.

  3. Review support facts: delivery time, shipping charges, returns, refunds, FAQs and the actual order-support route. Keep the source URL and last-check date for each policy. Do not add promises that the business cannot keep.

  4. Prepare answer guidelines: identify as AI representing the business; be helpful and clear; use verified facts and relevant product links; state uncertainty; do not invent stock, prices, reviews, credentials, fees or results. Do not claim to be an independent adviser or endorsed by OpenAI.

  5. Follow the current Business Agents Policy. Do not ask for unnecessary or high-risk personal data, pressure a customer, hide material conditions or give advice requiring a professional license. Use only supported, authorized features.

Test before asking to publish

  1. Preview with realistic questions: product suitability, price/stock, ingredients when relevant, shipping cost/time, returns/refunds, purchase link and order help. Test an unavailable item, an unknown fact and an out-of-scope question. Ask about a best seller only if sales evidence supports the answer.

  2. Compare every answer against current product pages and policies. Check links resolve to the intended page and material fees/conditions are stated. Log prompt, expected answer/source, actual answer, issue, fix and retest result. Fix errors and retest before publication.

  3. Current capability limit: the agent explains offerings/policies and links to the website. It cannot complete purchases, make bookings or check a customer's order in this experience. Do not configure promises that exceed this limit.

Publish only after approval

  1. Show the actual configuration, claims, remaining limitations and preview results for the user's final approval. A request to write this SOP is not approval to publish an agent.

  2. Only after explicit approval, select Publish, confirm the correct agent is live and test the customer-facing experience. Record the actual profile/agent URL if available, publication time and any unresolved issue. Claiming, catalog connection and agent publishing are separate outcomes.

Insights after claim and Persona approval

Local-business mentions have a different definition: the result is shown for that location. Discovery topics group themes, not individual questions or direct evidence of customer intent.

  3. Blank is not zero: data may be below threshold, still collecting or unavailable. Preserve the displayed state. Do not promise visibility, placement, sales or recommendation increases from a claim, feed or agent.

Proposed maintenance checklist

  1. Once authorized as a recurring task, review weekly and whenever products/policies change: prices, stock, new items, website links, delivery/returns/refunds, source information and sample agent answers. Business Hub does not expose individual customer questions; use authorized support FAQs and aggregate topics instead. Report changes and obtain required approval before external updates.

Completion record

Record each separately: correct account/profile selected; eligibility checked; claim submitted/approved; Persona submitted/approved; profile information approved/published; feed processed; insights availability; agent eligibility; tests passed; final publish approval; publication confirmed; follow-up owner/date. Use Pending, Not available or Not applicable rather than claiming an unfinished step is done.

Purpose and scope

  1. Build a traceable list of organic-search competitors for a chosen market, then distinguish direct business competitors from sites that merely rank for the same searches. Part C organizes original Tab 3. It supports selecting competitors for Part A, but organic ranking overlap is not the same as AI mention/citation overlap.

  2. Use five methods in order: Domain Overview, keyword-based SERP research, Keyword Gap, Position Tracking Competitors Discovery and manual Google results. Traffic & Market Analysis, Advertising Research and Backlink Gap remain excluded, as the source requests. A referring-domains value may be recorded if already present; it is not a sixth discovery method.

Prepare the research

  2. Use the approved Semrush account and access route. Check current limits before exports, new projects or tracking campaigns. Creating a project or adding tracked keywords may use limited account capacity; this SOP grants no spending or account-change authority. Use an existing correctly configured campaign when available.

  3. Create a dated workbook with the five tabs below. Keep untouched CSV/source exports separately from filtered working lists. Save evidence URLs and report dates. Do not overwrite raw research to make a final list look cleaner.

Workbook structure

  1. Domain Overview Export: domain, database/country, Com. Level, Common Keywords, SE Keywords, estimated Traffic, estimated Costs, report date and export scope/row count.

  2. Final Competitors: normalized domain, Direct / Organic / Indirect type, all discovery methods, target country, estimated organic traffic, organic keywords, common keywords, Authority Score where available, relevance rating/reason, evidence URL and Selected / Rejected / Review status.   3. Keyword Research: keyword, local volume, global/country volumes, KD, SF, intent, CPC with currency, trend, our position, country/device, source date and evidence link. Use Not available when a metric is absent.

11. Domain Overview and keyword SERPs

Method 1: Build the base competitor list

  1. Open Semrush > Domain Overview and enter our own domain. The source strategy starts with Worldwide for the overview. Treat that as orientation only; do not mix worldwide totals with target-country results. Verify this selector and path in the actual interface.

  2. Find Main Organic Competitors > View Details > Competitors. If that shortcut is absent, the official documented route is Organic Rankings > Competitors. Confirm our domain is still selected.

  3. Select the target-country database and let the report refresh. Record country, date and scope. The official report finds organic keyword overlap, not automatically direct commercial competitors.

  4. Export the Competitors table to CSV/Excel using the available Export control. Choose all available results within approved limits, not just the visible first page. Record actual exported count and any cap. Do not call an export complete when access returned only a subset. The Overview PDF is not a row-level CSV export.

  5. Make five descending top-10 views of the raw data using the source's labels: Com. Level, Common Keywords, SE Keywords, Traffic and Costs. Preserve each view or tag the rows by metric. These are sorting/shortlisting rules from the user's strategy, not confirmed named Semrush preset filters. Verify exact column labels in the live export.

  6. Combine the five lists and deduplicate normalized domains, retaining every discovery reason. Five top-10 lists provide at most 50 row slots and can yield far fewer than 50 unique domains. Validate business relevance before selection. The source target is 50 unique relevant base competitors; report the actual count and shortfall rather than padding with unrelated domains.

  7. If fewer than 50 qualify, mark the target unmet and ask whether to extend beyond the top 10 of each metric or use additional methods to fill the base target. Do not silently change this selection rule or invent a weighting formula. Later methods can add validated candidates without a fixed final total.

Metric interpretation

  1. Com. Level uses both competitors' keyword portfolios and their common keywords; it is not simply the count of common keywords. Traffic and Costs are estimates, not actual visits, revenue or the competitor's advertising budget. Preserve labels and units and avoid equating organic strength with business fit.

Method 2: Inspect our high-volume keywords

  1. Open Organic Rankings > our domain > Positions. Keep the agreed target country, device and date. Sort Volume from high to low.

  2. Select business-relevant keywords with target-country volume of 500 or more, as specified in the source strategy. Do not substitute global volume for local volume. This threshold is a project rule, not a Semrush requirement; log a shortfall instead of adding irrelevant broad searches.

  3. Open a selected keyword or the available SERP-preview icon, then SERP Analysis. The small screen icon/popup sequence comes from the source and needs live-interface verification. If absent, use only a verified equivalent report and record the difference.

  4. Review the top 10 organic ranking results, not ads or feature cards counted as organic positions. Record the keyword, each relevant domain and exact ranking URL, plus report freshness. Open candidate websites to verify products/services, audience and market fit.

  5. Preserve available popup metrics: global volume, country volumes, KD label/percentage, SF (SERP Features), intent, CPC/currency and trend. Sample numbers in the source are not current research. Missing metrics remain Not available.

  6. Keep YouTube, Instagram, Facebook, Amazon, Flipkart and other social/marketplace results out of the final direct-business list under this strategy. Still retain their raw SERP rows and exclusion reasons; they can compete for visibility without being eligible direct-business selections.

12. Keyword Gap and Position Tracking

Method 3: Keyword Gap

  1. Open Keyword Gap. Put our domain in the first You field and add up to four already known competitors. Select the agreed regional database, device/date where supported, and Organic keyword type. Select Compare.

  2. Inspect Missing, Weak and Untapped for relevant opportunities. Save the keyword and each compared domain's position, volume and KD where available. Preserve the raw CSV/Excel export and filters. Record counts only if the report supplies them or a documented calculation supports them.

  3. Look for repeated relevant keyword coverage by the compared domains. Open their sites to verify actual business fit before adding or confirming them in Final Competitors. Keyword Gap compares domains already entered; it does not automatically discover an unlimited new set of competitor domains. New candidates need another discovery source or a verified SERP lookup before being compared.

  4. Link each final domain to its supporting keywords, gap type and source report. Add a new candidate only once and append additional discovery methods to its existing record.

Correct gap definitions

  1. Shared: every entered site ranks. Missing: all compared competitors rank, but You does not. Weak: You ranks lower than all compared competitors. Strong: You ranks higher than all compared competitors. Untapped: You does not rank and at least one competitor does. Unique: You ranks and none of the compared competitors do. These are relative to the selected You domain and filters, not generic labels for any single competitor.

Method 4: Position Tracking Competitors Discovery

  1. With approval, open an existing correct project or set up Position Tracking for our website. Confirm country, local area, device and selected target keywords before using campaign capacity. Record configuration and collection status.

  2. Open Competitors Discovery in the Position Tracking dashboard. Review domains with repeated visibility across the tracked keyword set. A new campaign may not have usable data immediately; report pending collection, not a fabricated result.

  3. Open candidate websites and validate relevance. Record domain, relevant shared tracked keywords, Visibility %, estimated traffic and keyword positions where displayed. Save location/device/date and add Position Tracking as the discovery method.

  4. Interpret this as competition for the chosen keyword set and location, not the whole market. Do not compare its visibility percentage directly with Part A's AI visibility score or a different tracking configuration.

13. Manual validation and final selection

Method 5: Manual Google organic results

  1. Prepare searches for the site's actual products/services. Prioritize relevant commercial searches and the agreed volume criteria. For example, a home-storage business might inspect product-category and local-supplier searches; the source's stone/countertop searches are examples, not universal keywords.

  2. Check each query in the intended country/location context. Record search language, device, location setting/context and timestamp. If that context cannot be confirmed, label it unverified; do not claim a precise target-country ranking.

  3. Inspect the top 10 organic results. Record actual result URLs and positions, separately from ads, AI answers, local packs and other features. Note repeated domains, but do not assume repetition alone proves direct competition. Manual results can differ from a dated Semrush snapshot.

  4. Visit candidate sites and inspect product/service, audience and market fit. Apply the same social/marketplace exclusions to the final direct-business list. Preserve raw observations and reasons for exclusions.

Final validation and handoff

  1. Combine all methods in a working list. Normalize domains consistently, but do not merge distinct businesses or subdomains with different roles without checking them. Keep original ranking URLs and evidence attached.

  2. Rate business relevance: High = closely matching offer, audience and target market; Medium = partial overlap needing review; Low = weak/unrelated fit. This is a working review rubric, not a Semrush score.

  3. Assign Direct, Organic or Indirect and Selected, Rejected or Review separately. A publisher may be an organic competitor without selling a competing product. A social platform/marketplace can remain in raw visibility evidence while excluded from the chosen direct-business list.

  4. Save actual counts by method, unique count, rejection/review reasons and the Method 1 target shortfall if any. Preserve every discovery source and supporting keyword. Present the shortlist for approval before using it to make project decisions or claims about a particular business.

  5. Quality check: correct own domain/country; untouched exports; five metric views; 500+volume applied to relevant local keywords; top10organic results distinguished; gap meanings correct; tracking config captured; sites manually validated; duplicates removed; no sample values passed off as live data.
