SEMrush Brand Performance

Step-by-step: see how AI tools talk about the brand, in four reports (Brand Performance, Perception, Narrative Drivers, Questions)

1. Prepare the brand profile

Confirm the domain, target country and language with the project brief. Brand Performance needs one brand profile per domain, and the account has a limited number of profiles.

Working example, real run on 19 Nov 2026: skylinestoneproducts.com, United States, English, platform ChatGPT, data dated Nov 19 2026. Run through Jupiter Semrush Server 2 (Server 1 showed an upgrade prompt). The profile took about 10 minutes to fill; Semrush shows "Come back in 5-10 minutes" until then.

2. Open the report and set up the profile

Open Jupiter → Semrush (Server 2) → AI → AI Visibility → Brand Performance (/ai-seo/brand-performance/). Enter the domain, click Analyze, then in "Set up new brand profile" confirm Domain, Target location (United States) and Target language (English), and click Get started.

Perception (/ai-seo/perception/), Narrative Drivers (/ai-seo/narrative-drivers/) and Questions (/ai-seo/questions/) read the same profile. Open them from the AI Visibility menu after the profile is ready.

3. Read the Brand Performance overview

Check the competitor list Semrush chose, then Share of Voice, Overall Sentiment and Key Business Drivers. Record the platform and date shown.

Observed example: competitors Granquartz, Bb industries, Alpha professional tools, Park industries. Share of Voice: Granquartz 8%, Bb industries 5%, Alpha professional tools 3%, Park industries 3%, Skyline 1%, Other 80%. Overall Sentiment: Favorable 78%, General 22%. Semrush marked Skyline "Far behind".

Share of Voice is a share of AI answer mentions in the sample, not website traffic. Treat the competitor list as Candidate until checked.

4. Read the Key Business Drivers table

The table counts how often each driver was attributed to each brand. Open a driver to see the answers behind it.

Observed example (Skyline vs Granquartz): Digital Commerce Reach 5 vs 6; Advanced Material Coverage 4 vs 16; Service Reliability and Guarantees 2 vs 12; Professional Market Penetration 2 vs 10; Technical Support Differentiation 1 vs 4; Procurement Simplification none vs 14.

5. Use the Insights cards

Each report starts with AI-generated Insights. Use them as ideas, then check them in the data. Some AI overview boxes fail with "Couldn't generate an AI-powered overview"; click Try again or ignore them, the numbers still stand.

Observed example insights: "CNC Microsite, Bigger Voice" and "Dominate Porcelain Questions" (Narrative Drivers), "Beat 'Just Another Catalog'" (Perception).

6. Perception: read sentiment and its reasons

Open Perception. Review Competitive Perception by Platform, Favorable Sentiment ranking, Key Sentiment Drivers (Brand Strength Factors and Areas for Improvement) and Sentiment by Feature Category.

Observed example: Favorable Sentiment Granquartz 88%, Park industries 85%, Skyline 78%, Bb industries 72%, Alpha professional tools 68%. Strengths: reliable delivery and service, curated third-party brands (Cyclone, Distar, Tenax), one-stop stone tools, chemicals and sinks, professional trade focus. Improvements: limited depth of Skyline-branded CNC tooling, seen as regional or mid-size, awareness barrier ("just another catalog"). By category Skyline scored 100% on pricing and value, quality and service, but 50% on user experience and e-commerce and 50% on brand and market presence.

Only ChatGPT shows data here. Google AI Mode, Perplexity and Gemini show "Paid plan".

7. Narrative Drivers: read share of voice and sources

Open Narrative Drivers. Review Share of Voice by Platform, Mentions, Average Position and Top Domains by Citations.

Observed example: Skyline share of voice 1% (Granquartz 8%). Semrush's platform note: 13% on ChatGPT, lowest vs competitors. Top cited domains: granquartz.com 39 citations, obsidian-industrial.com 30, stonify.io 24, bbindustriesllc.com 24, en.wikipedia.org 18, skylinestoneproducts.com 8.

Cited domains are the pages AI trusts. Plan content, PR or partner mentions for the domains that AI already cites.

8. Questions: read what people ask

Open Questions. Review Topic Distribution, Query Intent Distribution and the questions under each topic.

Observed example: intent split comparison 46%, research 46%, support 8%, education 1%. Topics include supplier discovery, brand reputation and vendor comparison, logistics and pricing, tooling performance (blades, pads, CNC), support and training, chemicals and sealers. Sample questions: "Where do most stone fabrication shops buy their diamond blades and polishing pads?", "Who offers the best balance of price and durability for diamond blades used in stone fabrication?", "Which brands offer polishing pads that deliver a high gloss on granite but won't burn quartz?"

9. Turn findings into actions and save the record

For each gap write one action with a target page. Example: low Advanced Material Coverage (4 vs 16) → build a porcelain and UCS hub page; low CNC support mentions → publish 3 to 5 CNC case studies; question "matching diamond tools to stone types" → a blade selection guide.

Tracker columns: Research date | Domain | Country | Language | Platform | Share of Voice | Favorable sentiment | Top competitor | Weakest driver | Top cited domains | Priority questions | Action | Target page | Owner.

10. Final quality check

Brand Performance is a sample of AI answers on one date. No action guarantees that AI tools will mention the brand.

Final quality gate (tick karo)

  • Correct domain, country, language, platform and date.
  • Competitors checked for real product overlap.
  • Percentages labelled as AI sample shares, not traffic or GSC data.
  • Paid-plan platforms marked Not available, not zero.
  • Output: one brand baseline plus a list of content actions tied to questions and cited domains.

12.43.C Expected Output

Output: brand performance report - share of voice %, sentiment summary, improvement actions.
